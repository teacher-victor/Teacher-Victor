The Brief

Policy Briefing: Where Should Our Mission Invest Its Effort?

Your role: you are a political officer at the Permanent Mission of Arvenia (a fictional, mid-sized country) in New York. The Ambassador has fifteen minutes before a meeting with the capital and wants to know where the political team should invest its time next year.

Your task: study the three bodies and the four small charts, then give a three-minute spoken briefing that compares the options, admits the strongest point against your choice and recommends how to divide the team's effort. Use language from the previous Reading and Listening lessons (Lesson 5 and Lesson 6).

Permanent Mission of Arvenia to the United NationsRestricted · Political section

To: the Permanent Representative From: Political Officer Subject: Where to invest our effort next year

Ambassador, you asked where the political team should put its effort next year. We have twelve diplomats, or about 400 person-weeks, and three possible priorities: the Security Council, the General Assembly and the Human Rights Council in Geneva.

On paper, the Security Council is the obvious choice: its decisions are binding and it sits at the centre of every crisis. Admittedly, no other body has that power. That said, we are not a member, the Council adopted only 44 resolutions in 2025, and on the files we follow it is in gridlock. Last year we spent about 180 person-weeks there and shaped one text. A single veto can undo that kind of work in an afternoon.

By contrast, the General Assembly gave us our best results. Its resolutions are recommendations, not orders, but they carry legitimacy: every state has one vote. We were among the co-sponsors of nine texts, and our oceans resolution was adopted by consensus. Most of that work happened in informal consultations, at a cost of about 150 thousand euros.

Geneva sits in between. A seat on the Human Rights Council would give us a seat at the table for three regular sessions a year, and our team already follows four special procedure mandates. Travel makes it more expensive, and some partners still pay lip service to our priorities. Even so, our flexibility there brought us two new partners. Because we have no arrears, our candidacy is credible.

My recommendation: half of our effort to the Assembly, a third to the Geneva campaign, and the rest to the Council, mainly through contacts in the margins. Cutting the Geneva team completely is a red line for the capital. With respect to colleagues who prefer the Council, the numbers point to the Assembly.

Coaching Room

Frame, Contrast, Commit

This briefing compares three options that all have defenders. The fastest way to lose an ambassador is to ignore the option they like. This three-part framework builds the concession into the middle of your talk, so that your recommendation sounds fair, not one-sided.
PartPurposeTimeSignal phrases
FrameDefine the decision and the measure you will use to judge it (here: results for effort).30 secondsYou asked where ... / The fairest way to compare them is ...
ContrastWalk through the bodies using the charts. Concede the best point for the option you will not choose, then answer it.2 minutesAdmittedly ... That said ... / By contrast ... / Whereas ...
CommitGive one clear recommendation with proportions and one condition or red line. Invite questions.30 secondsMy recommendation is ... / The one thing we cannot do is ... / Happy to go deeper.

Model briefing

Frame

Ambassador, you asked where our political team should invest its effort next year. We have about 400 person-weeks to spend, and the fairest way to compare the three bodies is simple: what results do we get for that effort?

Contrast

Let me start with the Security Council. Admittedly, it is the only body whose decisions are binding, and on paper it is the place to be. That said, we are not a member. Last year we put 180 person-weeks and 420 thousand euros into it and shaped one text, in a year when the Council adopted only 44 resolutions. By contrast, the General Assembly cost us 120 person-weeks and 150 thousand euros, and we were co-sponsors of nine texts. Its resolutions are only recommendations, but with 193 votes they carry real legitimacy. Geneva sits in between: it is more expensive because of travel, and our influence score there is a team estimate, so I would treat it with some caution. Even so, it is our highest score, at seven out of ten.

Commit

My recommendation is to give half of our effort to the Assembly, a third to the Geneva campaign, and the rest to the Council, mainly through contacts in the margins. The one thing we cannot do is close the Geneva team; for the capital, that is a red line. I am happy to go deeper on any of the three.
