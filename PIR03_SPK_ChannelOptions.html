Scenario: The Republic of Aldana and its neighbour Karsia closed their embassies three years ago after a dispute over river water. Trade has collapsed and both governments blame each other in public. Privately, each side has signalled that it wants a way out. The Foreign Minister of Aldana must choose how to make first contact.
Your task: you are the Minister's senior policy adviser. In 3 to 4 minutes, brief the Minister on the three options: state your recommendation first, compare the options using the scorecards, name the main risk, and ask for a decision. Briefing memo

TO: The MinisterFROM: Senior Policy AdviserRE: First contact with KarsiaRESTRICTED

Minister, the bottom line first: I recommend Option B, a backchannel hosted by Norvale as facilitator. Official contact with Karsia has been deadlocked for three years, and neither government can afford to be seen to move first. A facilitated channel lets both sides test the waters behind closed doors, with a high level of deniability. If it fails, both leaders can save face.

Option A, a direct secret meeting, is faster. But without a host there is nobody to set ground rules, and nobody to keep the talks alive when we reach a sticking point. Option C, a conversation in the margins of the regional summit, is cheap, but more than forty people would know about it, so the risk of a leak is high. Reportedly, Karsia's hardliners are looking for any reason to block contact.

Norvale is said to have hosted similar talks before, and its diplomats are known to be discreet. I can't be certain that Karsia will accept, but its envoy appears to be interested. If you agree, we go ahead this week: a first meeting, a one-page non-paper, and an agreed press line in case of questions.

Rehearsal

The Minister's Four Minutes: Line, Lay Out, Weigh, Ask

A minister between two meetings does not want a story with a surprise ending. Give the answer first, then just enough evidence to trust it. Four moves, about one minute each.
MoveWhat you doLanguage to use
1 · LineState your recommendation in one sentence.The bottom line first: I recommend ...
2 · Lay outName the options, one line each. No detail yet.There are three options. Option A is ...
3 · WeighCompare on two or three criteria, with numbers. Name the main risk and how sure you are.A is faster, but ... / Reportedly ... / I can't be certain, but ...
4 · AskAsk for one decision, with a date.Do I have your approval to go ahead ...?

Model briefing

Line

Minister, the bottom line first: I recommend Option B, a backchannel hosted by Norvale.

Lay out

We have three ways to make first contact with Karsia. Option A is a direct secret meeting between two envoys. Option B adds Norvale as a facilitator. Option C is a conversation in the margins of the regional summit.

Weigh

Option A is the fastest: we could meet in two weeks. But B is the most deniable and the most durable, and it scores twenty out of twenty-five overall, against seventeen for A. Option C is cheap, but more than forty people would know, and the estimated risk of a leak is sixty percent. The main risk with B is time: four weeks. Karsia's envoy appears to be interested, although I can't be certain that his minister agrees.

Ask

So my request is simple. Do I have your approval to go ahead with Norvale this week? If so, I will prepare a press line before anyone travels.
