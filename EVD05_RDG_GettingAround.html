Grammar

Imperatives and Prepositions of Place and Movement

Why it matters: when you give directions, you tell someone what to do (Turn right. Don't cross.) and you say where things are and how to move (next to, opposite, along, past). With these two tools, you can help a visitor, or ask for help, anywhere.

1. Place or movement?

Prepositions of placePrepositions of movement
QuestionWhere is it?How do I get there?
MeaningThe position of a thing. It does not move.The way a person moves from A to B.
Examplesnext to, opposite, between, behind, in front of, above, on the corner ofalong, past, into, out of, up, across, towards
Typical verbbe: The hotel is opposite the library.walk, go, turn, drive: Walk along King Street.

2. The forms

Imperative = verb (no subject): Turn left.  ·  Don't + verb: Don't cross yet.
verb + preposition of movement + place: walk along King Street
UseFormExample from the chat
Give an instructionverb (base form)Turn right. Walk up Bridge Road. Take the number 4.
Say what not to dodon't + verbDon't cross the road yet! Don't take a taxi.
Make it friendlylet's + verbLet's meet at the Blue Door café.
Movementverb + along / past / into / out of / upWalk out of the station. Go past the bank. Turn left into Bridge Road.
Position (one thing)be + next to / opposite / behind / above / in front ofThe hotel is opposite the library. It's behind a big tree.
Position (two things)between + A + and + BThe café is between the bookshop and the bakery.
Why text messages use this grammar: directions in a chat are short and quick to read. Imperatives have no subject, so every message is one clear step. Prepositions replace long explanations: "opposite the library" is enough.
